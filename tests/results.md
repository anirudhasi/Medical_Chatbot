### Q1: What is anaphylaxis and what causes it?
Anaphylaxis is a serious allergic reaction that can begin very quickly and may be life-threatening. It is caused by the immune system responding to otherwise harmless substances from the environment. Common causes include reactions to foods (especially peanuts), medications, stinging insects, exercise, and exposure to latex, though sometimes no cause can be found.

### Q2: What are the symptoms of appendicitis?
The main symptom of appendicitis is pain in the abdomen, often on the right side, which is usually sudden and gets worse over time. Other symptoms may include swelling in the abdomen, loss of appetite, nausea and vomiting, constipation or diarrhea, inability to pass gas, and a low fever. Not everyone with appendicitis will have all these symptoms.

### Q3: How is Bell's palsy treated?
Bell's palsy is often treated with antiviral medications and/or steroids, although there is some debate about their effectiveness. Alternative treatments may include acupuncture, facial massage, chiropractic manipulation, and vitamin B12 supplements. Most individuals with Bell's palsy begin to see improvement within 2-3 weeks, and at least 80% fully recover.

### Q4: What is bruxism and what causes it?
Bruxism is the habit of clenching and grinding the teeth, often occurring at night during sleep but also possible during the day. It is an unconscious behavior that may be performed to release anxiety, aggression, or anger. Many people are not aware of it until their teeth have been damaged.

### Q5: What causes albinism?
Albinism is caused by a flaw in one or more of the genes responsible for directing the eyes and skin to make melanin, the pigment. It is an inherited condition, with each cell in the body containing a matched pair of genes, one inherited from each parent.

### Q6: What is aortic dissection and why is it dangerous?
Aortic dissection is a rare but potentially fatal condition where blood passes through the inner lining and between the layers of the aorta, creating an abnormal second channel. This condition is dangerous because it can lead to the closing off of connection points to other arteries, potentially causing heart attacks, strokes, abdominal pain, and nerve damage. Additionally, blood may leak from the dissection and collect in the chest and around the heart, further complicating the situation.

### Q7: What are the symptoms of bipolar disorder?
The symptoms of bipolar disorder involve mood swings known as mood episodes. The symptoms of a manic episode can include feeling very up, high, or elated, feeling jumpy or wired, being more active than usual, having a very short temper, or seeming extremely irritable, and having racing thoughts and talking rapidly.

### Q8: What is biliary atresia?
Biliary atresia is a condition where a fetus fails to develop an adequate pathway for bile to drain from the liver to the intestine. It is the most common lethal liver disease in children, occurring in about one in every 10,000 to 15,000 live births. This condition often leads to the need for liver transplants, as half of all liver transplants in children are done for this reason.

### Q9: How is angioplasty performed?
Angioplasty is a non-surgical procedure where a catheter with a balloon tip is threaded from a blood vessel in the thigh into the blocked artery. The balloon is then inflated, compressing the plaque and enlarging the blood vessel to open the blocked artery. This procedure is typically performed in a hospital's cardiac catheterization lab, and patients are usually awake but given medicine to help them relax.

### Q10: What is amnesia and what are its causes?
Amnesia is a loss of memory often traceable to brain tissue damage. It can be a symptom of several neurodegenerative diseases, but people with amnesia typically remain lucid and aware of their memory disorder. Causes of amnesia include brain injury from physical trauma, disease, infection, drug and alcohol abuse, or reduced blood flow to the brain, as well as conditions like Wernicke-Korsakoff syndrome and infections such as encephalitis and herpes.

### Q11: What are the risk factors for breast cancer?
The risk factors for breast cancer include a family history of breast cancer in a mother or sister, early onset of menstruation and late menopause, reproductive history such as having no children or having children after age 30, and never breastfeeding. Other factors include a history of abnormal breast biopsies, older age, a personal history of breast cancer or benign breast disease, and inherited risk such as BRCA1 and BRCA2 gene changes. Lifestyle and environmental factors, such as high-fat diets and obesity, may also affect the risk.

### Q12: What is athlete's foot and how is it treated?
Athlete's foot, also known as tinea pedis, is a fungal infection that affects the feet. It is typically treated with antifungal creams or sprays such as clotrimazole, ketoconazole, miconazole nitrate, sulconazole nitrate, or tolnaftate. If the infection does not respond to topical treatments, a doctor may prescribe an oral antifungal medication.

### Q13: What is anoxia?
Anoxia is a condition characterized by an absence of oxygen supply to an organ or a tissue. It results when oxygen is not being delivered to the affected area.

### Q14: What is a bone biopsy used for?
A bone biopsy is used to distinguish between malignant tumors and benign bone diseases such as osteoporosis and osteomyelitis. It may be ordered to determine the cause of bone aches or soreness, or when a mass or deformity is found on diagnostic imaging procedures like x-rays or CT scans.

Acute lymphangitis is characterized by painful, red streaks just below the skin. It is a bacterial infection in the lymphatic vessels. If you suspect lymphangitis, it is important to contact a doctor immediately or go to an emergency room.

### Q16: What is aortic valve stenosis?
Aortic valve stenosis is a condition where the aortic valve, located between the aorta and the left ventricle of the heart, is narrower than its normal size. This narrowing can restrict the free flow of blood from the left ventricle to the aorta.

### Q17: How are bites and stings treated?
Most bee and wasp stings can be treated at home by scraping off the stinger with a blade, fingernail, credit card, or piece of paper, cleaning the area, and applying an ice pack. Anticancer drugs, also known as antineoplastic drugs, are used to treat malignancies, which are cancerous growths. They help control the growth of cancerous cells and may be used alone or in combination with other treatments such as surgery or radiation therapy.

### Q19: What is an anoscopy and why is it performed?
An anoscopy is an examination of the rectum where a small tube is inserted into the anus to screen, diagnose, and evaluate problems of the anus and anal canal. ### Q20: What are arteriovenous malformations?
Arteriovenous malformations (AVMs) are defects in the blood vessels that occur before birth during prenatal development. These malformations involve blood vessels that lack the usual amount of smooth muscle tissue and elastin, leading to veins receiving blood directly from arteries at higher pressure than they are designed to handle. This can result in complications such as spontaneous hemorrhage or other serious issues.

### Q21: What is bartonellosis?
Bartonellosis is an infectious bacterial disease that can occur in an acute form, with a sudden onset and short course, or a chronic form, with a more gradual onset and longer duration. It is transmitted by sandflies and occurs in western South America. The disease is characterized by a form of red blood cell deficiency known as hemolytic anemia.

### Q22: What are bleeding varices?
Bleeding varices are bleeding, dilated (swollen) veins in the esophagus or the upper part of the stomach, caused by liver disease. They can be life-threatening since uncontrolled bleeding can lead to death.

### Q23: What is adrenal gland cancer?
Adrenal gland cancer, also known as adrenocortical cancer, is a type of cancer that occurs in the adrenal glands, which are located on top of each kidney. These glands produce important hormones such as adrenaline, noradrenaline, and cortisol. Diagnosis typically involves laboratory tests to measure hormone levels and imaging tests to visualize the tumor, and treatment may include surgery, radiation therapy, and chemotherapy.

### Q24: What are adjustment disorders?
Adjustment disorders are debilitating reactions to stressful events or situations, typically lasting less than six months. They are characterized by symptoms such as hopelessness, sadness, anxiety, worry, headaches, stomachaches, withdrawal, and destructive behaviors. These symptoms must significantly interfere with a person's social, job, or school functioning, and psychotherapy is the preferred treatment.

### Q25: What is balloon valvuloplasty?
Balloon valvuloplasty is a procedure performed on children and adults to improve valve function and blood flow by enlarging the valve opening. It is a treatment for conditions where the heart valves are too narrow, known as stenosis, specifically for aortic, mitral, and pulmonary stenosis. During the procedure, the patient is given an oral sedative such as diazepam (Valium).

Parkinson's disease is caused by a deficiency of dopamine, a neurotransmitter in the brain. The exact cause of this deficiency is not fully understood, but it may involve genetic factors or exposure to environmental chemicals. Most cases do not seem to run in families.

There is no cure for shingles, but antiviral medicines can help make the attack shorter and less severe. These medicines are most effective if taken within 3 days after the rash appears. They may also help prevent postherpetic neuralgia (PHN).

### Q30: What causes cystic fibrosis?
Cystic fibrosis is a genetic disorder caused by mutations in the CFTR gene. This gene mutation affects the lungs and digestive system, leading to the production of thick and sticky mucus. It is a recessive disorder, meaning a person must inherit two copies of the faulty gene, one from each parent, to develop the condition.
